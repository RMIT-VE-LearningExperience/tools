These files never leave this machine. There is no server behind this page. You can disconnect from the network and it will still work — which is a reasonable thing to do before you start.
What this is, and what it isn't. This is a check before you upload, not a guarantee of anonymity. It finds common patterns — emails, phone numbers, licence numbers, addresses — and asks you about names and employers. It cannot make a document anonymous. Your trade, your ticket combination, your employment dates and the kind of sites you've worked on may still identify you to anyone who knows the industry. Treat the result as personal information still.

Type it as it reads. Don't tidy the wording or expand abbreviations — an assessor needs the document, not an improved version of it. What you type is scanned for personal information the moment you stop typing, and shows in the panel on the right.

People

Names aren't detected automatically — pattern matching gets them wrong in both directions, and a missed name is the one failure that matters. Type every person who appears: the candidate, referees, supervisors, assessors.

Organisations

Don't blank these — describe them. An assessor judging your evidence needs to know the scale and setting you worked in. Tomago Aluminium → a large primary aluminium smelter (NSW) keeps what matters and drops what identifies. Dismiss anything that isn't an organisation.

Reduced copies
Everything involving an AI

Open the Candidate Toolbox, fill in your details once, and work through prompts A0 to A5, pasting these reduced files in where a prompt asks for your documents.

Why: whatever you paste into an AI goes to that company's servers, outside your control and your RTO's. The reduced copies keep your name, contact details and licence numbers out of it.

Originals
Your assessor, and no one else

Send your assessor the original testamur, licence, tickets and references — as issued, unaltered — alongside your finished handover pack.

Why: your assessor has to confirm your documents are genuine and yours. A reduced copy can't do that. They can't ring [PERSON_1] or check [USI_1] against the register.

isolation-key.json stays on this machine. Never upload it, never paste it into a chat, never email it. It is the only thing that turns the tags back into real names — and you'll need it at the end, to restore your handover pack before you send it.

Read a reduced file before you go. This tool checks for patterns; it does not understand your documents. If a name, a site, a licence number or anything else that points to you is still in there, come back and tag it.

They are still about you. Removing a name does not make a document anonymous — your trade, tickets, dates and work history remain, and together they may well identify you. Send them only to a service you would be willing to send your CV to.
